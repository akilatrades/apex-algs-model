    Did we get it right?

    Before trusting a percentage, check it. We rated teams using only earlier games, then looked at games the model hadn't seen: the Split 2 Playoffs and the second half of Pro League. We sorted every team-game by how likely we said something was, split them into five equal groups, and counted how often it actually happened.

    How we tested it

    We rated teams using only results from before the Split 2 Playoffs, then compared that with what actually happened there (63 games).

    Bars show the points a game each group actually scored. The teams we rated highest did score the most. One caution: we used this same event to fine-tune things, so it may do a little worse on new games. In Pro League, the team we rated higher finished ahead of the time.

    Ideas we tested

    Each idea was added to the model and scored on the same games it hadn't seen. "Better (+5%)" means its accuracy score went up 5%. We kept only what helped. The match-point adjustment is used when rating teams, so a good final isn't mistaken for a better team, but it adds no wins in the title odds (see What's new).

    What's new in this update

      What it doesn't know

      • The real Champs groups, format and map order, which haven't been announced. We use the Split 2 Playoffs format and map order.
      • Whether teams will move landing spots for Champs.
      • Ring 3 and later: the zone map uses rings 1 and 2, which settle most of the question.
      • Players who haven't played an official game together yet: their history only counts through their old teams.
      How it works, and where the numbers come from

      The ranking. Every game's full finishing order counts, not just who won. Finishing 3rd ahead of 17 other teams tells us something. Teams with only a few games are pulled toward the average for their region, so one lucky weekend can't send a team to the top. Teams who've never played a LAN get a small deduction, sized by how much first-time LAN teams dropped off last time.

      Players. Part of each rating follows the players, so a player who moves teams brings some of his record along. A brand-new trio starts from its players' history instead of from zero. Players who always played together can't be told apart, so for most teams this changes little.

      Landing spots and match point. The model also learns how much worse teams do when they share a landing spot with another team in that game, and how teams play once they're on match point. Learning the match-point effect separately keeps it from distorting the ratings.

      The odds. We play out the whole Championship 8,000 times: groups, the elimination rounds, then the match-point final, using each team's ranking plus a realistic amount of luck. A team's chance is simply how often it won. Groups are drawn at random, since the real ones aren't out yet.

      Checks. Every game's results and every team's totals match the stats site, including the site's own season totals for all team-and-event records. We also checked the maths behind the odds with a set of automatic tests.
